name: Product evidence authority
description: Authority boundary for internal packaged-product discovery before customer-facing integration.
---

**Current founder requirement:** One consistent product-recommendation safety standard must cover Grocery Coach, Find Product, Product Scan, Shopping List options and other grocery callers. Honor current dietary identity, allergies, avoided foods, medical nutrition needs and physician restrictions; identify the actual product and evaluate its evidence. Known conflicts are rejected; insufficient evidence is never verified compatibility. This supersedes any earlier allowance to describe unresolved products as suitable merely because they received a Profile-Matched Pick label.

**Why:** The founder explicitly clarified that the goal is shared recommendation safety across grocery features, not just Shopping warning cleanup. Cardiac soy-sauce advice should use existing medical guidance and actual sodium evidence; shrimp allergens and dietary conflicts must be excluded everywhere.

**How to apply:** Investigate and report the smallest reusable corrections before changing safety logic. Preserve ordinary shopping intent separately from suitability, reassess relevant profile changes, and never invent clinical thresholds.

**Standing development scope:** "Our only active development priority is completing and verifying the My Perfect Meals Shopping List functionality." Cover Grocery Coach, Find Product, Product Scan and Shopping List Product Options. Recommendations should help the client using their current dietary preferences, allergies, foods to avoid, medical needs, lab-work needs and protocols. Do not work on Spanish localization, Beverage Creator, Recipe Maker or other queued tasks. Keep unfinished Beverage work preserved separately. Development only; no commits, pushes, merges or deployments without explicit approval. Do not automatically start another task when this work finishes.

**Why:** The user explicitly limited active work to the complete grocery recommendation and Shopping List workflow.

**How to apply:** Keep verification and corrections within that workflow. Reuse applicable existing customer/physician guidance rather than rebuilding the clinical system or inventing new limits. Useful qualified recommendations are distinct from unsupported compatibility claims; known conflicts remain rejected.

**Rule:** Treat subject resolution, reviewed product-rule policy, and exact source evidence as separate gates. A condition name, support toggle, legacy provider prompt, or evidence template cannot itself create an executable clinical packaged-product restriction. An exact barcode record observed now establishes source identity, not current formulation; never transfer its observation date to its ingredient or nutrition facts. Unknown fact freshness and rule-specific thresholds remain unresolved rather than receiving convenience defaults. Product Scan's by-name or scanned-label verdict is not a catalog eligibility decision.

**Why:** MPM's established food rules are spread across the envelope, daily planning, Human Food, and shadow clinical authority. A correct evidence reducer can still produce incorrect safety claims if its caller invents who the subject is or what threshold applies. The Human Food Context fingerprint includes a fresh generation-chain ID and timestamps on every resolution; comparing fingerprints from two healthy reads caused a false “profile could not be verified” error. A universal unresolved identity-freshness policy made every Development catalog card say “check the label,” even for people with no active hard rule; exact record identity and formulation age are different questions. USDA publication/retrieval dates do not prove a current package formulation, and its public DEMO_KEY is rate-limited. Open Food Facts search can return irrelevant hits even for a specific query; its index is not package evidence. Product Scan's full-analysis prompt assumes a verified scanned label; catalog ingredients do not satisfy that premise. Development and Production share clinical data, so isolated tests must not be mistaken for live policy approval.

**How to apply:** Resolve the subject again for the actual decision, but never compare separate Human Food Context fingerprints for equality; compare only the safety-relevant search intent built from the first read and use the later resolved subject for all candidate decisions and commentary.

**Shared Development standard:** Every specific branded product presented as compatible must use the same exact-product checks against the customer's current requirements. An unresolved rule or insufficient evidence remains unknown, not a Profile-Matched compatibility claim. Useful source-backed comparisons and explicit shopping references may remain available without implying approval.

**Why:** The user approved one consistent verification standard across Grocery Coach, Find Product, Product Scan alternatives and Shopping List Product Options, rather than a second, less strict recommendation layer. They want useful personalization, not impossible guarantees about a changing physical package or a new clinical engine.

**How to apply:** Reject known conflicts and distinguish missing customer authority, missing product evidence and missing applicable rules. Never claim allergen absence, current-package clearance or daily low-carb compliance from catalog facts. A saved shopping identity and historical assessment are not current compatibility; retain identity, quantities, recipe provenance and original evidence when reassessment is needed. Do not use per-request generation identifiers to invalidate an unchanged safety context.

Product records must preserve original statements, serving basis, market/variant, source, observation and retrieval dates; AI suggestions and the legacy barcode `verified` boolean do not count as proof. For Open Food Facts fallback, filter index hits against the requested food and US market, then fetch the exact barcode and recheck identity before using any fact. Keep catalog interpretation fact-cited, bound to the exact subject decision, and non-decisional; never pass catalog text as a verified scanned label. Search past rejected or unverifiable candidates within a bounded budget, and never call an unresolved case eligible.

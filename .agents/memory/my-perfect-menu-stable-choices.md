---
name: My Perfect Menu stable choices
description: Product boundaries for idea retention, completed meals, Favorites, and replacing choices.
---

My Perfect Menu owns one stable working set of three ideas per category. Selecting an idea does not consume it. Returning after inspecting a completed meal restores the same three unless authoritative food or clinical context makes them stale. “Try 3 More” explicitly replaces the current three; choices never accumulate to six, nine, or fifteen.

**Why:** Users cannot judge whether they want to keep an idea until its governing Builder creates the complete meal. A second Menu-specific save system would duplicate Favorites and blur the difference between discovery, creation, planning, and retention.

**How to apply:** My Perfect Menu discovers, the resolved Builder creates and labels the completed meal, Favorites keeps it for later, and the Weekly Board represents the current plan. Builder navigation must carry an allowlisted return target with the originating Builder, category, authorized subject, and Performance destination when applicable; navigation alone never changes food authority. Teach users to Favorite a completed meal before deleting or replacing it, and confirm before replacing the current three.

Dessert replacement must handle incomplete historical structure explicitly, without inventing missing identity or treating every uncertain comparison as approval. Shared ingredients do not establish duplication when recognizable dessert structures differ.

**Why:** The user requires meaningful variety, including rejection of renamed repeats, without presenting flavor variants of one dessert as three distinct choices. They explicitly prefer making better use of generated candidates over increasing AI retries.

**How to apply:** Preserve accepted candidates, the five-round maximum and all safety checks. Keep existing choices on failure, and distinguish an unresolved history comparison from a medical rejection. Ordinary diagnostics must exclude patient context, prompts and generated descriptions.

Historical novelty is recipe-level, not a permanent prohibition on a dessert format. The current three should have different recognizable structures; older history must not make every future pudding, cookie or cake a duplicate merely because its format appeared before.

**Why:** Live replacement attempts exhausted their candidates when broad historical format matches were treated as conclusive repeats. Missing identity evidence must still remain explicit rather than being fabricated.

**How to apply:** Compare historical preparation and composition, preserve rejection of renamed recipes, and keep the Try 3 More confirmation consistent with the app's black-glass, white-text modals.

Presentation limits must not discard otherwise inspectable food concepts or remove original food evidence from later validation.

**Why:** Repeated live failures included candidates rejected solely for overlong descriptive copy; shortening it before checking safety or losing it on restoration would hide potentially meaningful food details.

**How to apply:** Keep full original evidence through initial validation, persistence, restoration and Builder handoff. Short display copy is separate; never use it to bypass any protection or increase the retry ceiling.

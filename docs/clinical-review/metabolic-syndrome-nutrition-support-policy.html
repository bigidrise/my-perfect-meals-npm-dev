2. Scope, identity, and non-negotiable boundaries

Core proposal: metabolic syndrome should improve food quality and personalization across human food tools. It must not introduce a GLP-1 overlay, automatic portion reduction, or a universal forbidden-food list.

Metabolic syndrome is a clinical cluster involving abdominal adiposity, blood pressure, glucose, triglycerides, and HDL cholesterol. It does not establish that every component is present, diagnose diabetes, prove medication use, or prescribe a particular macronutrient ratio. Diagnostic background is supported by S8; this policy does not implement diagnostic criteria.

  • Proposed independent condition identity: metabolic-syndrome, with display label “Metabolic Syndrome.” The final identifier and normalization rules require review. Never reuse glp1 or automatically reinterpret existing GLP-1 records.
  • Record user-reported condition information as user-reported. Do not label it clinician-verified without genuine verification. Do not infer a diagnosis from measurements, medication, appearance, or the selected Builder.
  • Preserve saved calorie and macro targets, explicit recipe-design instructions, serving count, serving preferences, dietary identity, allergy rules, cultural preferences, and legitimate medical care plans. Recommendations must not silently save replacement targets.
  • Do not force weight loss, high protein, low carbohydrate, a GLP-1 Builder assignment, a smaller meal, or a new mandatory onboarding stage.
  • Scope is human food personalization. Pet nutrition, medication prescribing, diagnosis, exercise prescriptions, supplements as treatment, and emergency treatment design are outside this policy.
  • For professional workflows, use the exact authorized client and organization/Location context, not the practitioner's personal profile. Household subjects need their own authorized context.
  • Adults are the proposed initial evidence population. Pediatric, pregnancy, frailty, eating-disorder, undernutrition, and other medically complex cases require their established specialized policies and clinical review; do not extrapolate an adult weight-loss pattern.

Enforcement classes

  1. Prefer: rank or generate suitable choices consistent with the approved dietary pattern.
  2. Limit: propose a reasonable reduction or substitution and explain it. This does not mean “never eat.”
  3. Independent safety constraint: enforce a genuine allergy, dietary identity, authorized patient-specific clinical restriction, or established safety rule. The metabolic-syndrome label alone creates none of these hard stops.
  4. Uncertain: identify missing composition or clinical evidence. Do not claim exact nutrient compliance from a dish name, model estimate, or unverified product record.

Rules operate on the whole eating pattern where evidence is available. A single food is not equivalent to an entire diet. Clinical recommendations and product-preservation requirements must remain visibly distinguishable in the implementation.

Each clinical rule below has a source ID and a bounded interpretation. Quantities and exceptions must be reviewed before any numeric implementation. Preserving an established preference does not guarantee that every dietary pattern optimally manages metabolic syndrome; explain genuine tradeoffs without silently changing the person's diet.

Product-preservation requirements, not new clinical thresholds

MS-P1: portion sizes, servings, and saved nutrition targets remain governed by the user's established setup and individual circumstances. MS-P2: preserve the requested dish's identity and make suitable substitutions instead of default rejection. MS-P3: keep clinical and dietary safety independent. MS-P4: disclose what changed and why, with rule/source traceability. These are explicit product requirements; their implementation must not be presented as a newly discovered medical guideline.

Substitution examples

  • Shake: use a compatible unsweetened milk or plant alternative and suitable fruit/fiber ingredients, rather than adding avoidable syrup. Preserve serving size and protein target; no rule requiring every shake to be small.
  • Soup: offer reduced-sodium broth and suitable herbs rather than rejecting soup. Do not claim compliance with a numeric limit if product sodium is unknown.
  • Dessert: reduce avoidable added sugar or replace some saturated fat only when the recipe remains workable and recognizable. Do not replace a requested cake with fruit and call that equivalent.
  • Restaurant: suggest an available preparation or sauce adjustment when supported by the menu. State that sodium/composition are uncertain when no nutrition or ingredient evidence is available; do not invent numbers.
  • Restrictive dietary identity: use compatible choices first. If the diet meaningfully conflicts with the recommended pattern, disclose the tradeoff and offer optional clinical discussion rather than covertly switching diets.

4. Conflict-resolution approach

This is proposed precedence for the new support layer, not permission to rewrite established clinical policies. Conflicting clinical instructions require clarification; “the most restrictive rule wins” is not a safe universal strategy.

  1. Resolve the exact authorized food subject and applicable care context. Never borrow the practitioner's targets or another household member's condition.
  2. Preserve genuine allergy/food-safety requirements and established acute-care exceptions.
  3. Apply current, authorized patient-specific clinical instructions with known provenance. If instructions conflict, identify the conflict and avoid claiming clinical compliance; request qualified review where needed.
  4. Apply existing dietary identity and explicit meal/recipe intent within those safety boundaries.
  5. Apply metabolic-syndrome preferences and substitutions where compatible. Keep portion count and saved targets unchanged.

Missing composition must be disclosed; existing high-risk safety policies determine whether an item can be offered, needs clarification, or cannot be cleared.

S1 — Primary current dietary-pattern guidance

Organization/authorship: American Heart Association; Lichtenstein et al. Title: 2026 Dietary Guidance to Improve Cardiovascular Health: A Scientific Statement From the American Heart Association. Publication: Circulation, May 5, 2026, 153:e1285–e1295; ahead of print March 31, 2026.

Original publisher • DOI: 10.1161/CIR.0000000000001435.

Supports: MS-01–MS-08: whole dietary patterns; varied vegetables/fruits; whole grains; suitable protein; replacing saturated-fat sources with unsaturated-fat sources; minimally processed foods; less added sugar and sodium; no encouragement to start alcohol. General cardiovascular-risk population, not a metabolic-syndrome-specific numeric prescription.

Currency: explicitly supersedes the AHA 2021 dietary-guidance statement. Use 2026 as the proposed primary citation. Do not silently delete or replace any historical reference already displayed in the app.

Independent verification for S1: the original publisher text states: “This scientific statement supersedes the ‘2021 Dietary Guidance to Improve Cardiovascular Health: A Scientific Statement from the American Heart Association’.” The independent NCBI PubMed record, PMID 41914202, confirms the exact 2026 title, journal, DOI, and March 31, 2026 electronic publication date. The publisher identifies the final issue as May 5, 2026. These are different publication stages, not conflicting dates. Its nine food-pattern features directly support the bounded recommendations listed in MS-01–MS-08; they do not establish individual portion caps or universal food bans.

S2 — Condition-specific institutional guidance

Organization: National Heart, Lung, and Blood Institute, NIH. Title: Metabolic Syndrome — Treatment. Last updated: May 27, 2022.

Original source.

Supports: MS-01, MS-03–MS-05, MS-08. Heart-healthy lifestyle is first-line; DASH may be appropriate; fruits, vegetables, and whole grains are emphasized, with limits on saturated fats, sodium, added sugars, and alcohol. Organization: NHLBI, NIH. Title: DASH — DASH Eating Plan. Last updated: February 25, 2026.

Original source.

Supports: MS-01–MS-07: vegetables, fruit, whole grains, compatible lean/plant protein sources, lower saturated fat, and fewer sugar-sweetened beverages; lower-sodium preparation. For example, a lower-added-sugar shake must actually omit/reduce the avoidable syrup while preserving its requested serving count; a fat substitution must change the actual suitable fat source; a soup's numeric sodium claim needs matched ingredient/serving evidence. Unchanged safe food is a valid outcome. Capture representative real generator results for review; canned/mock outputs prove validation wiring only, not model or clinical acceptance.
T04 — Food identity and availabilityOrdinary desserts, homemade shakes, snacks, and restaurant choices remain available when otherwise safe. A cake stays a recognizable cake. No category-level ban, mandatory small portion, zero-carbohydrate rule, or daily-budget refusal is introduced.
T05 — Dietary identities and allergiesVegan, vegetarian, celiac/gluten-free, low carb, carnivore, kosher/halal, and allergy cases preserve their actual rules. A whole-grain substitution cannot introduce gluten; a protein substitution cannot introduce dairy/fish into vegan food; an unsaturated-fat substitution cannot introduce a known allergen.
T06 — Clinical coexistenceExercise Type 1/Type 2 diabetes, prediabetes, current glucose-dependent constraints, existing hypoglycemia-care exceptions, CKD stages/dialysis, cardiovascular restrictions, pregnancy, pediatric and undernutrition contexts. No inferred diagnoses, high-protein defaults, potassium-salt defaults, unsupported limits, or conflicting instructions hidden behind “approved.”
T07 — Subject isolation and clinical ownershipPersonal, household, and authorized professional client paths use the correct subject. Client/org/Location switches discard stale evidence and late responses. Unauthorized client access remains denied; no actor-to-client target copying. Self-reported condition removal must not erase clinician-owned protocols or lab evidence. Verify current authority after save and reject stale-context results without granting new clinical access.
T08 — Whole-surface final-output coverageFor every surface in section 5, prove context threading and inspect final-result validation. Include actual meal ingredients/preparation, completed beverage/shake ingredients and serving basis, Menu concepts and completed recipes, dessert branches, grocery alternatives, restaurant estimates, retries, repair, fallback, cached/restored candidates, and newly recommended saved items. After an authorized profile change, repeat representative meal and beverage operations and verify the refreshed condition context rather than an old cached choice. Imports or prompt keywords are not sufficient.
T09 — Nutrition evidence and uncertaintyDistinguish whole-recipe from per-serving quantities, added sugar from total sugar, ingredient evidence from dish names, and label facts from estimates. Unknown sodium cannot pass an exact sodium-compliance assertion. Product facts remain bound to exact brand/variant, serving basis, source, and date.
T10 — GLP-1 non-regressionMetabolic syndrome alone does not activate GLP-1 targets, tolerance, fat ceilings, or portion caps. Existing GLP-1 code/records are not altered. When both contexts are legitimately present, independent authorized Builder/Menu support and other clinical rules compose without spreading new quantity restrictions.
T11 — Citation integrityEvery clinical rule references an existing approved source and locator. Verify original title, organization/authors, publication versus update date, DOI/URL, supported population, and review status. No invented citations or applicability claims. Confirm AHA 2026 supersession is represented without deleting existing sources.
T12 — Sources UIFrom the real Dashboard and Macro Calculator entry points, open the existing panel; verify the new section, complete reference cards, rule links, external URLs, keyboard/screen-reader labels, mobile scrolling, no horizontal overflow, and accurate approval wording. Preserve all existing references. Test link failures without replacing citations silently.
T13 — Source accessibilityCheck canonical links with bounded requests, redirects, DOI resolution, and content/title identity—not HTTP status alone. Distinguish transient network failure, access restrictions, and genuinely unavailable content. A publicly accessible abstract is not a full-methods review.
T14 — Release boundaryDraft remains inactive until review. Verify intended API registration and frontend visibility in the actual production build before release; confirm no Development-only guard silently removes approved references. Confirm no account/data backfill, payment, entitlement, or historical-recipe mutation is introduced.

Unit and contract tests should establish deterministic behavior and provenance. A single focused browser pass should cover the eventual changed onboarding/Sources flow and representative final food output. Authenticated clinical acceptance must use authorized evidence; fixtures may demonstrate wiring but cannot be described as live patient-data verification. Nutrition-therapy claims require clinical review in addition to software tests.

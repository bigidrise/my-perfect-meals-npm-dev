# My Perfect Meals — Customer Impact & Clinical Safety Assessment

**Development source assessment · November 11, 2026**

## Executive recommendation

**Correct the English-only safety communication on five high-priority surfaces before the next release intended for non-English customers.** These are the Hydration Center's urgent sick-day guidance, GLP-1 symptom intake, professional hydration prescriptions, saved-grocery safety qualifications, and the Nutrition Life Plan's incomplete allergy-profile warning. The untranslated wording is confirmed in source; misunderstandings, incorrect entries, and downstream harm are **potential consequences, not observed incidents**.

**No P0 defect was established by this investigation. No incorrect calorie calculation, changed nutrient restriction, allergy bypass, unintended charge, or broken save was demonstrated by these 92 localization failures.** This is a bounded source-review conclusion, not certification that the application is safe or fully functional. No authenticated workflows were browser-tested.

The ten protected-source mismatches require controlled reconciliation. Five concern superseded Academy headings, three concern replaced Learn explanations, and two concern changed GLP-1 profile qualifications. They are not ten mistranslated clinical instructions. The changed GLP-1 profile qualification and diabetes education deserve medical/content review; do not regenerate approvals or restore obsolete prose just to clear the check.

After the five priority surfaces, repair clinical intake comprehension and important account, meal-saving, shopping, and MFA instructions. Defer branding, transient loaders, developer/synthetic interfaces, and scanner false positives. Do not raise localization allowances.

### Clinical-domain conclusions

- **Allergy/ingredient restrictions:** none of C01–C10 contains a changed allergen list. A separate active component finding, R09, does contain an English-only incomplete Alpha-gal profile warning. R02 contains English-only qualifications on historical product safety evidence. These deserve priority without assuming the backend protections failed.
- **Diabetes:** C04–C06 concern educational descriptions, not executable glucose thresholds. R08/R06/U52 concern labels for carbohydrate categories, not demonstrated arithmetic changes. Current education's freshness/clinical-equivalence language needs review.
- **GLP-1:** C09–C10 weaken or replace explicit local qualifications and need controlled review. U31's untranslated symptom and fluid-tolerance choices can affect the accuracy of user input. No medication dosage instruction or numeric nutrition target was changed by these mismatches.
- **Cardiac/kidney/liver:** no direct numeric restriction change appears in the ten mismatches. U46's active hydration maximum/minimum choices are consequential for clinician-entered fluid guidance, including fluid-sensitive patients. The risk is misunderstood meaning/units; a reversed limit was not observed.
- **Oncology:** U07/U08 expose English-only symptom intake wording. Shared symptom options also originate outside the component scanner's local hits. This is an intake-comprehension risk, not evidence that treatment or oncology protocols changed.
- **Physician control:** U39 is explicitly development-only shadow review, and its copy says current meal rules are unchanged. Do not mistake its unlocalized “at most/at least” controls for proof that live clinical limits were altered. U46 is a different, active professional hydration workflow and must not be dismissed as developer-only.
- **Clinical translation approvals:** the reproduced failures are source-integrity mismatches, not reports of ten incorrect foreign-language translations. The 0/446 migration warning is additional governance context, not a 93rd failure and not permission to ship unreviewed translations.

## 4. The five P1 priorities: exact evidence and proposed correction

All five are **confirmed English-only source defects with potential safety consequences**, not runtime-confirmed injuries or demonstrated bypasses. Escalate these communication risks to the founder and clinical localization reviewer now. If these workflows are offered to customers relying on other supported languages, do not treat them as cosmetic cleanup.

### P1-A — U02: sick-day emergency guidance and temporary instructions

- File: `client/src/components/HydrationFourDoorPanels.tsx`.
- Evidence: lines 68–79 contain hardcoded symptom choices including inability to keep fluids down, fainting/confusion, breathing/chest pain, and blood in vomit/stool. Lines 354–377 render the sick-day section and escalation. Line 371 says to seek urgent medical help/local emergency guidance. Lines 424–525 qualify user-entered temporary instructions and confirmation.
- Workflow: `/hydration` → Sick-Day or Liquid Nutrition Support. `Router.tsx:480–548` requires the hydration-center entitlement, presents a Pro upsell when unavailable, and offers basic Biometrics tracking instead. This is not an internal-only component.
- Impact: NE readers can miss urgent escalation or mistake user-entered instructions for professionally verified instructions. EN wording and escalation logic are not shown to be incorrect by this audit.
- Correction: clinically reviewed translations of the entire symptom/alert/instruction unit, not just the detected 47 hits. Preserve symptom IDs, escalation predicates, original quoted care instructions, dates, source attribution, and activation rules.
- Verification: every urgent symptom must show the correct localized urgent message; non-urgent selections must not falsely escalate. Check Spanish, Tagalog, Arabic RTL, long-text/mobile, and review unverified-instruction confirmation without activating real records.

### P1-B — U31: GLP-1 symptom intake changes the data the app receives

- File: `client/src/components/glp1/GLP1MealPreflight.tsx`.
- Evidence: lines 68–73 severity choices, 188–225 appetite/symptom/vomiting/fluid/eating questions, and 233–238 save controls are English literals. `save()` at lines 120–139 submits the selected canonical values. The 10 scanner hits substantially undercount the English on this form.
- Workflow: `client/src/pages/MyPerfectMenu.tsx:1183–1206`, personal Menu when GLP-1 is active or the selected builder is GLP-1, excluding a separately selected client subject. Context resolution/preflight handling appears around lines 405–477. Actual route: `/foods-i-enjoy`, through `GuardedMyPerfectMenu`/PaywallGuard (`Router.tsx:802,1281`); the component name is not the route.
- Impact: NE users may misunderstand severity, select “none”/normal defaults, or incorrectly answer whether they can retain fluids. This is not just an untranslated title. Incorrect submission and resulting nutrition changes were not observed.
- Correction: controlled translation of every question, option, status, action, and applicable escalation message, preserving canonical values (`cant_keep_fluids`, `with_difficulty`, etc.), loading guards, and submission semantics.
- Verification: assert translated labels submit identical canonical payloads; distinguish inability to keep fluids from nausea; confirm current-record loading protection and relevant escalation after a controlled synthetic submission.

### P1-C — U46: clinician hydration minimum/maximum meaning

- File: `client/src/components/pro/ProHydrationControls.tsx`.
- Evidence: lines 216–224 render “Daily target/range/minimum/maximum” and target/minimum/maximum ounces. Lines 116–124 build English directive summaries. `saveClinical()` at lines 127–147 maps the selected kind and converts ounces to milliliters. The trainer-only warning around lines 238–241 prohibits prescribing volumes/electrolytes and dehydration tactics.
- Workflow: `/pro/clients/:id/clinician`, mounted at `ClinicianClientDashboard.tsx:805–808`; the trainer variant is mounted at `TrainerClientDashboard.tsx:805`. Both require appropriate Studio/client access. Not development-gated at these mounts.
- Impact: NE professionals can confuse a minimum with a maximum, or misread units/scope; clients may receive a materially wrong intended fluid instruction if an entry is mistaken. No incorrect conversion or persisted directive was demonstrated.
- Correction: medically reviewed target-kind, bound, unit, daily-scope, date, and trainer-boundary translations. Keep internal kind values and ounce-to-milliliter conversion unchanged. Do not invent clinical target values.
- Verification: synthetic point/range/floor/ceiling round trips; minimum/maximum semantics in each reviewed language; unit labels correspond to the existing conversion; trainer copy never implies prescribing permission.

### P1-D — R09: incomplete allergy-profile warning

- File: `client/src/components/protocol/NutritionPersonalizationSummaryCard.tsx`.
- Evidence: lines 380–410 render Alpha-gal dairy/gelatin statuses and the hardcoded warning “Profile incomplete — update in Edit Profile for full protection.” Lines 217–219 also render a care-team update entry.
- Workflow: personal `/dashboard`; also provided-summary professional nutrition views through `TrainerClientDashboard`, `ClinicianClientDashboard`, and `ProClientNutritionPlan`.
- Impact: NE users can miss the fact that allergy details still need verification. EN users also see the phrase “full protection,” which merits review because completion of a software profile is not proof of complete real-world protection. This investigation did not establish missing backend allergy blocks.
- Correction: clinically reviewed localization of the whole incomplete-profile/status unit, preserve incomplete state and Edit Profile navigation, and review the “full protection” claim for a more factual statement about completing allergy details. Do not assert an unreviewed replacement is approved.
- Verification: incomplete Alpha-gal state shows the warning in the selected language, “verify” is distinct from tolerated/avoided, and navigation opens the correct subject/profile.

### P1-E — R02: saved product history is not current safety approval

- File: `client/src/components/shopping/SavedGroceriesSheet.tsx`.
- Evidence: lines 600–619 render saved/current/history status, “Historical analysis is not current safety approval,” certification qualification, and current-label/retry actions. Line 623 exposes Add Product only for approved current evidence, not a historical version.
- Workflow: shopping → Saved Groceries → saved analysis/history. `ShoppingListMasterView.tsx` imports and opens this sheet; route `/shopping-list`. Exposure depends on saved evidence and analysis support, not a developer-only filename.
- Impact: NE readers may mistake saved analysis/certification history for present safety clearance or miss the current-label action. The source retains a current-approved add gate; no bypass or unsafe product addition was demonstrated.
- Correction: clinically reviewed translation of current-versus-historical status, approval/certification disclaimers, evidence-version choices, and verification actions as one unit. Preserve the existing add gate and evidence history.
- Verification: switch current/history/blocked/profile-unavailable fixtures in reviewed languages; historical or unapproved evidence must not acquire Add Product merely because labels were localized.

## 10. English and other-language impact

### English-speaking customers

The majority of these failures are not English-language defects: hardcoded English remains readable to an English speaker, and a failing ratchet does not execute at runtime. Specific English concerns needing review are:

- C05: “right now” / “real-time clinical precision” may overstate what a logged glucose value establishes.
- C09–C10: replacement of explicit local nutrition-only/non-substitution wording with a different legacy-setting explanation.
- R09: “full protection” is stronger than a software profile can establish on its own.
- R05: explanatory tour copy describes a particular compliance calculation and “every meal” shaping. This audit did not reconcile those broad claims against every current score/generator. Treat as a content-verification question, not a confirmed calculation defect.
- C06: the registry stores a truncated fragment; a rendered truncated sentence was **not** confirmed.

No demonstrated English sign-in, meal-save, payment, arithmetic, or navigation blocker was found from these localization findings. That limited result must not be represented as a full English regression test.

### Customers using other supported languages

Hardcoded rendered strings are unaffected by `i18n.changeLanguage()`. Having matching locale keys does not translate literals, and an English fallback configuration does not fix a literal that never calls the translation function.

Confirmed source defects therefore include mixed-language navigation and forms; English emergency warnings, symptom/limit controls and safety qualifiers; English meal destinations and replacement confirmations; English recurrence/scope disclosures; and untranslated accessible labels. The audience is conditional on the routes/roles/states above, not every user on every visit.

No professional linguistic review of all 13 languages, Arabic mobile layout/RTL check, screen-reader run, or customer-comprehension study was performed here. This report does not certify existing translations' medical fidelity merely because key parity passes.
